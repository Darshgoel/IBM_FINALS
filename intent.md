### 🍱 Food Access & Recovery Network

Food insecurity and food waste coexist because food supply, community demand, and distribution resources remain fragmented and poorly coordinated.

Communities may face food shortages while restaurants, event organizers, supermarkets, NGOs, and other organizations simultaneously have surplus food or unused resources. NGOs themselves can also experience mismatches, where one organization has excess food, volunteers, storage, or transportation capacity while another faces a shortage. Existing food-donation platforms primarily focus on connecting individual donors with recipients, rather than addressing this larger, continuously changing ecosystem.

We propose **FoodBridge**, an AI-powered food access and recovery network that connects food need, surplus, and community resources. The system will help identify and predict where food shortages and surpluses are likely to occur, enable organizations to collaborate and share resources, and intelligently coordinate the movement of suitable food toward communities where it can have the greatest impact.

FoodBridge will incorporate a **human-in-the-loop safety and verification approach**, where technology assists organizations in assessing and documenting food for redistribution while authorized personnel remain responsible for final safety decisions. When direct redistribution is not appropriate, the platform can help identify suitable recovery pathways to reduce unnecessary waste.

Our goal is to move beyond simply asking **“How much food can we donate?”** and instead address **“How can we continuously reduce the gap between food available and food needed?”**

By connecting beneficiaries, food providers, NGOs, volunteers, logistics resources, and recovery partners into one coordinated network, FoodBridge aims to create a more responsive, efficient, safe, and sustainable food-access ecosystem.
