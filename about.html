About FoodBridge

A bridge between good food and real need.

Edible food is thrown away every day while families nearby go hungry. FoodBridge makes the handover simple, traceable and dignified — connecting surplus kitchens with verified community organisations that know their neighbourhoods best.

The FoodBridge principle

Good food belongs on plates, not in bins.

One network for donors, community organisations and coordinators — built around safe handovers and measurable outcomes.

Verified partners Safety-first matching Dignity by design

Why we exist

Surplus is not waste. It is a meal waiting for a route.

A hotel kitchen closes for the night with trays of fresh biryani. A community kitchen twenty minutes away is preparing dinner for families who have had a difficult week. Both have what the other needs, but without a shared system the food goes into a bin and the need goes unmet.

FoodBridge closes that gap. Donors list surplus with the details that matter for safety — quantity, food type, preparation state and the safe-to-eat window. The network then identifies a suitable verified partner using dietary fit, distance, urgency and receiving capacity.

Every step is recorded: the listing, the match, the pickup and the completed handover. That record is what makes the impact figures on this site real rather than promotional, and it is what helps coordinators improve the network over time.

Verified by people, not paperwork alone

Organisations are reviewed before they can request or receive food. Verification records are kept in the admin portal.

Close to the need

Distance, dietary requirements and receiving capacity are considered together, not as an afterthought.

Honest by default

Impact totals come from completed handovers. Unavailable data is labelled as unavailable — never presented as a live result.

Rescue first. Waste last.

What guides us

Three commitments shape every handover.

They are simple to state and deliberately hard to skip.

Food safety comes first

Listings include preparation state, storage requirements and a safe-until time. Matches that cannot be collected safely are not treated as successful.

Dignity is not optional

Recipients find food through a discreet public food finder. Access instructions are clear, and no one needs to prove hardship to see what is available.

Impact must be measurable

Completed handovers become the source of truth for meals rescued, people served and estimated CO₂e avoided. No test records are promoted as public impact.
