How It Works

From surplus to a shared meal — with safe recovery built in.

The network handles coordination so donors can give quickly and community teams can focus on people, not paperwork. NGOs and food banks meet demand; biogas partners handle eligible recovery routes when food cannot safely go to people.

Built into every match

Safety, distance and capacity are checked together.

No single factor decides where food goes. The network looks at the whole handover.

The journey

One continuous record from kitchen to community.

Each step is visible to the people who need it — and to nobody who doesn't.

01

A donor posts surplus

The donor adds the food name, quantity, preparation state, dietary tags, pickup window, storage needs and a safe-until time. A listing can be created in under a minute.

Donor dashboard → Post Surplus
02

The matching engine finds a fit

Nearby verified NGO and food-bank demands are ranked using food fit, urgency, receiving capacity and route time. Unmatched edible quantities may go to food banks; matches must arrive within the safe window.

Automatic, not a public race for food
03

The receiving partner confirms

An NGO records the completed handover. A food bank confirms receipt, which adds the received quantity to its inventory so in-date stock can meet later demand.

NGO or Food Bank dashboard → Matches and inventory
04

The handover is completed

Completed human-food handovers count toward meal impact. If donor surplus or food-bank stock expires, is unsafe, or cannot reach people within its safe window, the system removes it from human-food availability and assigns it to an eligible biogas facility for separately recorded recovery.

Food-bank receipt and biogas intake are recorded separately

Safety & trust

What the network will not pretend.

Food rescue only works if the food is safe and the impact is real. FoodBridge is deliberately conservative about both.

Verification is not automatic

Donors, NGOs, food banks and biogas operators are reviewed before their operational features are enabled. The portal shows a pending state until approval.

Impact excludes test data

Public totals are calculated from completed handovers and filtered to exclude test/QA records. They are not projections or marketing estimates.

Accepted is not delivered

An accepted listing is not counted as rescued food. It only becomes impact when the recipient confirms the handover.

Common questions

Do I need an account to find food?
No. The public food finder is open and does not require a login. You can search by area, see open access points and read current collection details.
How is a food recipient chosen?
The matching engine ranks compatible NGO and food-bank demand using food safety, dietary fit, urgency, capacity and route time. Food banks can also supply eligible in-date inventory to another partner's demand.
Can I choose which partner receives my food?
The standard flow matches supply automatically to an eligible NGO or food bank. Unmatched edible surplus may be offered to a food bank; the network intelligence portal gives coordinators visibility when a match needs review.
What happens if food cannot be collected in time?
The system checks expiry throughout the food flow. Expired or unsafe food is removed from human-food availability and routed to an eligible biogas facility. If no real eligible facility can accept it, the item is flagged for coordinator review; it is never counted as a completed meal handover.
Is my personal information shown publicly?
No. Public endpoints expose aggregate impact and open access-point details, not donor contact records, tokens or internal allocation data.
